# Event judgment

Select one listed option for its supported consequences. A confident explanation does not make an uncertain choice safe.

## Decide once

1. Read all options and the event's actual constraints. Separate explicit effects from plausible interpretations. Use current-run feedback for learning, never a memorised event title, number, sequence, or answer.
2. Exclude options explicitly involving deception, concealment, unauthorised disclosure, unfair blame, or bypassing a required safeguard when a feasible option avoids them. Evaluate conduct, not positive labels. Honest disagreement, proportionate escalation, negotiation, and ambitious work are not automatically unsafe. If every option has a drawback, choose the least harmful feasible one and preserve its uncertainty.
3. Apply `threat-control`: address an evidenced immediate threat and avoid increasing unresolved exposure. Ask whether downside can actually be contained with the resources and follow-up available. Unknown downside is not zero; do not describe confidentiality, reversibility, or future support as guaranteed unless the text supports it.
4. Among viable options, prefer durable work results, capability, useful relationships, and preserved wellbeing. Use the current `focus` as a tie-breaker, not permission to sacrifice every other dimension. With unclear focus, prefer supported benefits across multiple dimensions and fewer irreversible dependencies.
5. Compare the leading choice with its strongest feasible alternative. If no material evidence distinguishes them, choose the lower-exposure option; do not manufacture exact probabilities or numerical utilities. Commit without cycling through the same arguments.

## Carry the delivery target into the story

When a half-year review is approaching or a weak review remains active, compare the viable options on the work that will actually be finished, the person who can act on it, and the time or relationships it will cost. Prefer a feasible fix with an agreed handoff or a concrete update when it meets the same obligation with less exposure. When recent effort has failed to raise Output from a similar starting point, treat a promised gain from extra hours as uncertain. Protect delivery already achieved. If the work is urgent and extra effort is the only credible way to complete it, account for the cost and act. Use only the options actually offered.

## Before submitting

Check: listed choice, relevant warning addressed, no invented safety guarantee, affordable downside, next follow-up still possible. Note the decisive evidence and a short directional expectation, with unknown effects marked unknown. Do not print an essay or rehearse every book.

After the next observation, distinguish execution from effect. Carry any new warning into the ledger immediately. If feedback contradicts the expectation, revise the belief using `feedback-learning`; do not retroactively rewrite the old prediction as a fact. An appealing immediate gain does not establish that delayed costs disappeared.
