# Feedback learning

Keep observations separate from explanations. Learn enough to change the next decision without rebuilding a theory of the whole hidden engine.

## A compact checkpoint

1. Compare current Skill, Network, Output, Health, Dignity, and Wealth with the saved quarter baseline and recent action results. Record observed direction, not a claimed causal formula. Salary, events, losses, delays, and review settlement can all change net values.
2. Read the actual review grade, promotion result, and any explanation. Preserve explicit feedback about weak delivery, collaboration, capability, wellbeing, or accumulated risk. A weak grade is evidence to improve the next feasible period; a high grade without promotion does not identify an unpublished promotion threshold.
3. Choose one PROVISIONAL growth focus from the most specific relevant feedback and trends. Prefer a directly named shortcoming over a guessed bottleneck. With inconclusive evidence, keep balanced development and vary one purposeful investment instead of declaring Skill the only obstacle.
4. Write the diagnosis, next allocation change, and what observation would support reconsideration. Expire the focus at the next quarter checkpoint; review earlier for a promotion, serious warning, or contradictory result. A promotion also prompts a fresh diagnosis because requirements increase. Never lock a focus until the next promotion.
5. Reset the quarter baseline only at the next quarter's first allocation observation, after comparing with the previous baseline. Carry the review feedback and unresolved concerns forward between checkpoints.

## Diagnose a plateau

If a value stays flat despite effort, record `plateau; cause unknown`. Consider offsetting losses, delayed feedback, ineffective action, or a wrong hypothesis. Use immediate before/after observations when available to separate those possibilities. Do not call the dimension full, sufficient, capped, or worthless without explicit published evidence. Skill, Network, and Output have no published finite maximum.

If the same allocation repeatedly yields little observed progress, change one meaningful element at the next feasible opportunity: a different action for the same need, a complementary capability, or prevention of a visible loss. Preserve protection. A single noisy outcome can suggest a test but cannot prove a hidden rule or make the original choice irrational. If all plausible explanations remain uncertain, label them uncertain and make the more robust feasible choice.

## A weak review opens a delivery check

Take the grade from the formal half-year bonus/review. Separate the grade from manager tone, promotion and story events about grading a colleague. A promotion can coincide with a weak review. After a weak grade or an explicit warning about delivery, carry one delivery target into each upcoming quarter and intervening stories until the next formal review. Keep the focus current by recording the visible loss to remedy (such as work undone or a missed handoff), the next feasible change, and the observed result. A review already shown has settled.

At the same level, compare a work action's pre-action Output with the next public observation and the current starting value. A costly action that missed its target while Health fell is evidence against buying it again under similar conditions. Carry one short comparison in the ledger. If feedback is mixed with a month change, incident or review, mark the cause uncertain. Reassess when the starting value drops, the menu changes or a promotion occurs. A local plateau is neither a published ceiling nor a reason to abandon development.

## Keep learning bounded

Do one checkpoint at each relevant change, then act. Do not reread the handbook or all logs each turn. Record only the current diagnosis and next check; no growing catalogue of event answers, fitted thresholds, or numeric success probabilities. A final score is retrospective evidence, not permission to start another game. This Skill neither launches tests nor changes the submitted files during play.
