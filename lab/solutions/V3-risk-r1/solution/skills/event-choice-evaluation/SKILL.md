# Event Choice Evaluation

Use for story events and follow-ups. `decision-mind` supplies pressure and risk judgment; `career-survival-core` executes. Quarterly decisions belong to `quarterly-planning`.

## Evaluate the current event

1. Read the latest state, relevant warnings and **every** offered choice before settling on one. Use only the handbook and current-run information, not event recognition or answers remembered from earlier runs.
2. Translate each option into useful effects, total cost and uncertainty. Remove flattering, frightening or urgent labels: equivalent consequences deserve equivalent treatment. Treat promises as uncertain unless supported; missing information does not mean zero risk. Current explicit effects override the cue table.
3. Apply `decision-mind` and check the strongest alternative. Identify the best reason your preferred option could be worse; change the choice if the evidence warrants it. Keep this brief on routine turns, using written comparison only for the shared warning, gamble and Health/Dignity triggers. Do not choose merely because an option sounds professional, avoids discomfort or appears first.
4. Return the number exactly as offered, a short decisive trade-off and predicted effect in the core's existing note format. After execution, check the prediction at the next observation. Do not create another action call or game loop.

## Wording-to-effect cues

These are hypotheses, not guaranteed effects or answers. Unknown effects stay unknown; do not invent magnitudes or probabilities.

| General cue | Possible benefit | Cost or uncertainty to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extra work or urgent delivery | Output, visibility | Health, repeated burden, recovery |
| Learning, mentoring, broader responsibility | Skill, Network | Delivery cost, capacity, time to benefit |
| Presenting real results, negotiating fair pay or resources | Visibility, Wealth, capacity | Evidence, commitments, relationship cost |
| Paid development or relationship activity | Skill, Network | Wealth cost, cheaper alternatives |
| Recovery or workload boundary | Health, Dignity | Foregone Output, delay |
| Concealed problems or bypassed safeguards | Apparent immediate gain | Deferred harm, hidden risk |

## Opportunities and follow-ups

A money or visibility opportunity may beat the quieter alternative when its credible benefit exceeds an affordable, recoverable cost. Neither avoiding every loss nor chasing a past setback is the goal; compare the future career states.

At follow-ups, reassess the remaining choices. Past effort does not justify further loss; future obligations and consequences still count. Carry forward a short evidence-based correction when a prediction fails. Do not turn one outcome, a vivid story or a confident explanation into a universal rule.
